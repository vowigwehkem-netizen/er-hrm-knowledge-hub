Landmark Judicial Precedents & Case Studies

Key rulings from the National Industrial Court of Nigeria (NICN) shaping employment practices.

CONSTRUCTIVE DISMISSAL

Miss Omolara Adewumi v. Guaranty Trust Bank Plc (NICN/LA/122/2012)

Fact Summary: The employee was subjected to severe pressure, humiliating environment, and forced demotion without due procedure, leading to her involuntary resignation.

Judicial Holding: The NICN held that where an employer's conduct renders working conditions so intolerable that an employee is compelled to resign, it amounts to Constructive Dismissal. The court awarded damages for wrongful termination.

Precedent: Resignation under coercion is actionable unfair dismissal in Nigeria.
TERMINATION & REASONING

Skye Bank Plc v. Anaemem Iwu (2017) LPELR-43595(SC)

Fact Summary: Employer terminated employee contract relying on traditional common law right to terminate "for good, bad, or no reason" by giving notice or pay in lieu.

Judicial Holding: Under Section 254C(1)(f) of the 1999 Constitution (as amended), the NICN has jurisdiction to apply international best practices and ILO Convention 158. Employers must now give valid operational or conduct reasons when terminating contracts.

Precedent: Standard termination now requires valid justification under international standards.

Comparative Global Labour Law Framework

Comparative analysis of employment relations legislation across jurisdictions.

Jurisdiction Contract Requirement Notice Period & Termination Dispute Resolution Forum
🇳🇬 Nigeria Written contract within 3 months (Labour Act Cap L1). Statutory 1 day to 1 month depending on tenure. Reason required under NICN practice. IAP $\rightarrow$ National Industrial Court of Nigeria (NICN).
🇬🇧 United Kingdom Written statement of particulars on or before Day 1 (Employment Rights Act 1996). Statutory minimum 1 week per year worked up to 12 weeks. Strict unfair dismissal protections after 2 years. ACAS Conciliation $\rightarrow$ Employment Tribunal (ET).
🇺🇸 United States At-Will employment dominant; written contracts rare except executive/unionized. No statutory notice required under "At-Will" doctrine unless governed by WARN Act or CBA. EEOC $\rightarrow$ State / Federal District Courts.
🇬🇭 Ghana Written contract required for employment lasting 6 months or more (Labour Act 651). 14 days to 1 month notice depending on payment cycle. Fair cause strictly required. National Labour Commission (NLC) Ghana.
🇿🇦 South Africa Written particulars mandatory upon commencement (Basic Conditions of Employment Act). 1 week to 4 weeks statutory notice. Strict substantive and procedural fairness enforced. CCMA $\rightarrow$ Labour Court $\rightarrow$ Labour Appeal Court.

🇳🇬 Nigerian Employment Relations Statutory Acts

Labour Act (Cap L1 LFN 2004)

Governs manual/clerical workers. Written contract required within 3 months, 12 weeks maternity leave, 12 days annual leave with pay.

Pension Reform Act (PRA 2014)

Applies to 15+ workers. Minimum contribution 10% (Employer) & 8% (Employee) managed by PFAs under PenCom.

Employees' Compensation Act (ECA 2010)

Provides compensation for occupational illness, injury, or death. 1% payroll fee paid entirely by employer to NSITF.

Trade Disputes Act (Cap T8 LFN 2004)

Outlines statutory conflict resolution: Internal Negotiation $\rightarrow$ Mediator $\rightarrow$ Conciliator $\rightarrow$ IAP $\rightarrow$ NICN.

Disciplinary Hearing SOP Playbook

Pillars of Natural Justice: 1) Audi Alteram Partem (Hear the other side), 2) Nemo Judex In Causa Sua (No bias).

    Issue written query stating specific charges and giving 48-hour response window. Review reply; if unsatisfactory, issue notice of Disciplinary Committee Hearing. Convene panel with neutral chairperson; allow employee to present defense & call witnesses. Issue written decision (exoneration, written warning, suspension, or termination).
