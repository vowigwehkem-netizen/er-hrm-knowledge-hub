Origins & Background

The Historical Evolution of ER & HRM

How modern management and industrial relations developed from the Industrial Revolution to the globalized knowledge economy.

ERA 1: INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION & WELFARE WORK (Late 18th - 19th Century)

The Birth of Labor Administration

With factory systems replacing cottage industries, harsh working conditions led to labor unrest. Early "Welfare Officers" were appointed in British and American factories to look after housing, feeding, and basic health of workers, marking the rudimentary beginning of personnel management.

ERA 2: SCIENTIFIC MANAGEMENT (Early 20th Century)

F.W. Taylor & Efficiency

Frederick Winslow Taylor (1911) introduced Scientific Management, focusing on time-and-motion studies, task specialization, and economic incentives. Concurrently, Henri Fayol established general administrative management principles.

ERA 3: THE HUMAN RELATIONS MOVEMENT (1930s)

Elton Mayo & Hawthorne Studies

Conducted at Western Electric’s Hawthorne Works, Elton Mayo’s studies proved that social groups, psychological factors, and worker morale significantly impact productivity more than physical working conditions alone.

ERA 4: STRATEGIC HRM & MODERN ER (1980s - Present)

Integration with Business Strategy

Personnel management evolved into Strategic HRM (SHRM), treating employees as strategic assets rather than variable costs. Simultaneously, Employment Relations formalized legal frameworks, tripartite negotiations, and international labor standards (ILO).

Human Resource Management (HRM) In Full Detail

Covering all core pillars of human capital management, organizational behavior, and strategic development.

1. Job Analysis, Design & Workforce Planning

The foundation of HR. Job analysis involves systematic data collection via observation, interviews, and questionnaires to produce **Job Descriptions (JDs)** and **Job Specifications (JSs)**. Job design applies models like the *Hackman & Oldham Job Characteristics Model* (skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, feedback) to enhance motivation and prevent burnout.

2. Talent Acquisition, Sourcing & Onboarding

Strategic recruitment balances internal mobility with external talent sourcing. Selection utilizes competency frameworks, psychometric assessments, and structured behavioral interviews (the **STAR method**—Situation, Task, Action, Result). Effective onboarding ensures cultural socialization and reduces early turnover.

3. Performance Management Systems

Evaluates individual and team output against corporate goals. Frameworks include **KPIs** (Key Performance Indicators), **OKRs** (Objectives and Key Results), **BARS** (Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales), **MBO** (Management by Objectives), and **360-Degree Feedback**. Underperforming staff are managed through structured **Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)**.

4. Total Rewards, Compensation & Motivation Theories

Encompasses direct pay, benefits, and intrinsic rewards. Job evaluation methods like the **Hay Point System** establish internal equity. Motivation is guided by classical theories: *Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs*, *Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory* (hygiene vs. motivators), *Vroom’s Expectancy Theory*, and *Adams’ Equity Theory*.

5. Learning & Development (L&D)

Identifies performance gaps through Training Needs Analysis (TNA). Implements programs using the **ADDIE model** (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) and evaluates training effectiveness using **Kirkpatrick’s Four-Level Training Evaluation Model** (Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results).

Employment Relations (ER) In Full Detail

Covering industrial relations theories, trade union dynamics, collective bargaining, and workplace justice.

1. Theoretical Frameworks of Industrial Relations

Examines how workplace authority and conflict are managed. Key perspectives include the **Ununitarist Perspective** (harmony of interests between management and staff), the **Pluralist Perspective** (organizations contain competing interest groups and trade unions are legitimate), and the **Radical/Marxist Perspective** (inherent conflict of interest between capital owners and labor).

2. Trade Unions & Collective Bargaining

Trade unions represent worker interests regarding wages, hours, and safety. **Collective Bargaining** is the negotiation process between employers (or employer federations) and recognized unions resulting in a binding **Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA)**.

3. Industrial Conflict & Dispute Resolution

When negotiations stall, industrial action occurs (strikes, work-to-rule, picketing by employees; lockouts by employers). Dispute resolution statutes mandate structured procedures: internal grievance channels $\rightarrow$ mediation $\rightarrow$ statutory conciliation $\rightarrow$ arbitration $\rightarrow$ labor courts.

4. Workplace Discipline & Natural Justice

Disciplinary actions must strictly satisfy the two pillars of natural justice: *Audi alteram partem* (right to a fair hearing and defense) and *Nemo judex in causa sua* (rule against bias). Arbitrary sackings or procedural breaches lead to legal liability for wrongful termination.

Worldwide Jurisprudence

Global Country Labour Acts & Statutes

Comparative breakdown of core statutory labor acts governing employment relations across major world economies.

🇳🇬 NIGERIA

Key Statutory Frameworks

  • Labour Act (Cap L1 LFN 2004): Governs manual/clerical workers; mandatory written contract within 3 months, paid annual leave, maternity provisions.
  • Pension Reform Act 2014: Mandatory contributory scheme (10% employer, 8% employee) for companies with 15+ workers.
  • Employees' Compensation Act 2010: No-fault injury insurance managed by NSITF (1% payroll paid by employer).
  • Trade Disputes Act & NICN: Governs union disputes and National Industrial Court adjudication.
🇬🇧 UNITED KINGDOM

Key Statutory Frameworks

  • Employment Rights Act 1996: Core statute covering written statements on Day 1, redundancy rights, and statutory notice.
  • Equality Act 2010: Consolidates anti-discrimination laws protecting 9 protected characteristics.
  • Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992: Governs union recognition and balloting rules for strikes.
🇺🇸 UNITED STATES

Key Statutory Frameworks

  • Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA): Regulates federal minimum wage, overtime pay eligibility, and child labor.
  • National Labor Relations Act (NLRA / Wagner Act): Guarantees workers the right to unionize and engage in collective bargaining.
  • Title VII of Civil Rights Act 1964: Prohibits workplace discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.
  • At-Will Employment Doctrine: State-level common law rule allowing termination at any time for any legal reason.
🇿🇦 SOUTH AFRICA & 🇬🇭 GHANA

African Jurisprudence

  • South Africa Labour Relations Act (LRA 1995): Promotes economic development, social justice, and statutory conciliation via the CCMA.
  • South Africa BCEA (1997): Establishes baseline working hours, leave, and termination standards.
  • Ghana Labour Act 2003 (Act 651): Comprehensive code covering fair termination, occupational safety, and the National Labour Commission (NLC).

Landmark Judicial Precedents

Adewumi v. Guaranty Trust Bank Plc (NICN)

Established that forcing resignation through intolerable conditions constitutes Constructive Dismissal under Nigerian jurisprudence.

Skye Bank Plc v. Anaemem Iwu (Supreme Court)

Empowered the NICN to apply international labor standards (ILO Convention 158), requiring valid justification for contract terminations.
